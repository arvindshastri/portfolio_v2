# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, recruiters and team leads in both software engineering and product design, plus founders and peers. They land on the site from a résumé, LinkedIn or a referral, usually on a laptop between other tasks, sometimes on a phone. They want to know quickly what Arvind has built, how he thinks, and whether he has taste. Recruiters in a hurry need a fast path to the résumé and contact.

## Product Purpose

A one-page portfolio for Arvind Shastri, a product designer and software engineer (McMaster Software Engineering, co-founder of Trac, previously Ericsson and CGI). The whole site is a single interactive object: an original click-wheel music device ("Pocket") that visitors operate to browse work, read long-form case studies, play music and a small game. Success means visitors remember it, spend time playing with it, read at least one case study, and leave convinced he can ship both engineer-grade code and designer-grade interfaces.

## Brand Personality

Tactile, nostalgic, meticulous. Playful without being a gimmick. The voice is warm and direct, first person, never salesy. Emotional goals: delight on first touch ("oh, it actually clicks"), then confidence once the case studies are read.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-generated portfolios: centered hero over a gradient, three equal cards, eyebrow labels on every section, cream backgrounds.
- His previous portfolio: neo-brutalist cards, hard shadows, indigo accent, content scattered across several pages.
- Over-busy concept sites where the gimmick hides the work (a cluttered desk scene, a 3D room you must explore).
- Copying other people's signature ideas (for example, "regenerate the site in any style").
- A literal iPod clone: the device is inspired by click-wheel players, not a replica.

## Design Principles

1. One object, done perfectly. Everything lives on or around the device; nothing competes with it.
2. Controls teach themselves. Physical metaphors (spin, press, hold) map directly to navigation, with light first-visit guidance.
3. Read without friction. Long-form case studies must be comfortable to read with a single action (press to open, which zooms in).
4. Delight is earned, not sprinkled. Music, Brick, notifications and unlocks are rewards for exploring, never obstacles to the work.
5. Works in the hand. The device should feel native on a phone, with the wheel under the thumb.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Full keyboard control (arrows, Enter, Escape, Space). Respect prefers-reduced-motion with instant or crossfade alternatives. Sound is opt-in by user action and never autoplays. Provide a plain text version of all content for screen readers and quick scanning.
